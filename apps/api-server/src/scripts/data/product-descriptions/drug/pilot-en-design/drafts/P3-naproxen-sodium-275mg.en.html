Over-the-counter Pain & inflammation

Naproxen Sodium 275 mg TabletA long-acting anti-inflammatory painkiller

Taken by mouth · Adults

This medicine is used for osteoarthritis, rheumatoid arthritis and ankylosing spondylitis; tendinitis and bursitis; acute gout; period pain; muscle and bone problems such as sprains, bruises and back pain; pain after surgery or a tooth extraction; and migraine.

What it helps with

  • Osteoarthritis, rheumatoid arthritis, ankylosing spondylitis
  • Tendinitis and bursitis
  • Acute gout
  • Period pain
  • Sprains, bruises and back pain
  • Pain after surgery or a tooth extraction
  • Migraine

At a glance

How it works

Fever, pain, inflammation

Reduces fever, relieves pain and calms inflammation.

Main symptoms

Aches and pains

Period pain, headache, toothache, muscle pain, joint pain, back pain.

Why this one

Chosen when longer-lasting relief of pain with inflammation is wanted.

Who should be careful

Stomach, heart, kidney

Stomach problems, heart or kidney disease, pregnancy.

Category

Over-the-counter

Available without a prescription at the pharmacy counter.

How to take it

For pain, take two tablets (550 mg) to start, then one tablet (275 mg) every 6–8 hours. Do not take more than 1,350 mg in one day.This medicine can upset your stomach, so take it after food if your stomach is sensitive. Do not take it together with another anti-inflammatory painkiller.

Before you take this

  • Do not take this if you have ever reacted to this medicine, to aspirin, or to another anti-inflammatory painkiller.
  • Do not take this if you have a peptic ulcer, severe liver or kidney problems, or heart failure.
  • Do not take this for pain around coronary artery bypass surgery (CABG).
  • Do not take this if you are pregnant or breastfeeding, or if you are 2 years old or under.
  • There is a risk of blood clots in the heart and brain (heart attack, stroke), and of bleeding, ulcers or perforation in the gut. Stop taking it and get advice if you have stomach pain, black stools, a rash or difficulty breathing.
  • If you drink alcohol regularly there is a risk of stomach bleeding — talk to a pharmacist before taking it.
